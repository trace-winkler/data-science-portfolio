## Blog 2 -- 9/7/2026
Recently, I've been working on a project that would allow me to see just how much the popularization of "two-high shell" defense has affected how NFL teams draft at the quarterback position in the first two rounds of the NFL draft. In the 2010's, defensive coordinator Vic Fangio reintroduced two-high safety to NFL defenses, which basically mad it harder for teams to pass the ball down the field. If you can't pass the ball as far, you're more likely to rely on rush plays and short throws. Anyways, I wanted to see if NFL teams became more likely to draft "scrambling" or rushing quarterbacks because of this. It made sense to me, as having a dual-threat quarterback would make it much easier for teams to pick on the weaknesses of two-high safety. So far, I've only created a visualization that shows the percentage usage of two-high safety vs one-high safety from 2015. I wanted to get information from further back, but it only became an official statistic in 2015, so that was all I had to go off of. I'm super excited to finish up this project though, as I believe that it will help give me a deeper understanding behind the thought process of NFL front offices.
